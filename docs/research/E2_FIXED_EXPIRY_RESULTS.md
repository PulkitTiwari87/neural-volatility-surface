# Experiment E-2 results: fixed-expiry target versus the E-1 nearest-expiry target (executed 2026-10-13)

> **REAL SPY end-of-day option data (Kaggle-hosted; upstream OptionsDX licence unverified). DESCRIPTIVE results only.** No significance, interval, calibration or trading claim is made or supported ([KNOWN_LIMITATIONS](KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md) L-22). Backtest only. Numbers were read from the log of a committed private Kaggle run; E-1 ([E1_RESULTS](E1_RESULTS.md)) and the frozen Phase 0 evidence are unchanged.

## 1. Question and verdict

E-1 ended with an untested hypothesis: *much of the short-tenor error comes from the label switching to a different real expiry between the origin and the target date, so a same-expiry label would be easier.* E-2 tests it.

**Verdict: the hypothesis is not supported; it is contradicted for the persistence baseline.** On the identical sample of (origin, cell) pairs where both labels exist, persistence is **harder** under the same-expiry label (RMSE 3.128 vs 2.700 vol points), and the gap is concentrated at the 7- and 14-day tenors (4.715 vs 3.822 and 2.776 vs 2.615) and vanishes at 60 days and beyond, where the two labels coincide. **No model is shown to improve on persistence**: the GRU seed-average is marginally below persistence in RMSE under the new label (and above it in MAE); the PCA-ridge advantage seen under E-1 disappears. Changing the label changes model rankings but produces no consistent improvement.

## 2. Target definitions (what exactly differs)

Both labels use the same surfaces, features, origins `t`, target rows `t+h` (h = 1 trading day), folds and models. The label is the 1-day change `y(t+1, c) - s(t, c)` of a grid cell `c` = (tenor, log-moneyness node).

| | E-1 `nearest_expiry` (existing) | E-2 `fixed_expiry` (new) |
|---|---|---|
| How `y` is chosen | on each date the real expiry **nearest the tenor** within about one day (or 1.5 % of the tenor) anchors the cell; `y` is that expiry's IV at the node on `t+1` | the expiry that anchored `c` on **`t`** is looked up on `t+1`; `y` is **that same expiry's** IV at the same node |
| Expiry selected from | target-date quotes (independent on each date) | **origin-date quotes only**; the future is read only to form the label |
| Expired / missing expiry on `t+1` | not applicable | label **missing and counted** (censored, never imputed) |
| Eligible (primary) cells | observed on `t+1` (tight quotes) with a real value on `t` | anchored on one real expiry on `t` with tight quotes **and** the same expiry tight on `t+1` |
| Maturity of the cell | re-anchored every day (about constant) | shortens by one day (**roll-down is part of the label**) |
| `s(t, c)` and features | identical | identical |

Code: `features/panel.py` (`fixed_expiry_targets`, `anchor_switches`, `prepare(..., target=)`), `surfaces/grid.py` (anchor expiries and per-expiry slices recorded per date), `evaluation/compare.py`. I implemented a **fixed-expiry** (contract-tracking at slice level) label rather than a fixed-strike single-contract label: tracking one exact option contract would multiply missing quotes in the wings, whereas the slice-level label keeps the same node grid and the same quote-quality rules and censors only when the expiry has no usable slice. A true constant-maturity target and a maturity-adjusted fixed-expiry target were **not** run.

**Leakage, look-ahead and survivorship controls (all tested):** the anchor expiry of row `t` is computed from row-`t` quotes only (test: scrambling every later quote leaves rows up to `t` identical); features at an origin are invariant to later rows (leakage audit passed on the real panel: 1,730 origins identical, tabular and sequence); folds are chronological with purged boundaries (`assert_no_overlap` passed in all 8 folds); fill values, scalers, PCA, ridge penalty and early stopping use training rows only. Survivorship: cells whose expiry has no usable slice on the target date are **censored and reported** (4.9 % of anchored cells), never dropped silently; the causes of censoring (expired, too few quotes, node outside the quotes) were not decomposed.

## 6. Comparison against E-1 (what changed and what did not)

- **Unchanged:** E-1's headline result reproduces exactly; under the nearest-expiry label no neural model beats persistence and only PCA-ridge is marginally ahead.
- **New:** the same-expiry label is harder for every model (pooled RMSE about 3.0 vs 2.6), so the E-1 hypothesis that expiry switching was inflating the error is **not supported**. The persistence error is concentrated at the shortest tenors (and is larger for a rolling contract than for the re-anchored label), not removed by following one contract.
- **Model ranking is label-dependent and unstable:** PCA-ridge +0.044 (nearest) vs -0.003 (fixed); GRU-ensemble -0.085 vs +0.014. A model that appears to help under one label does not under the other, which argues against reading either margin as skill.
- **Where the GRU gains under the fixed label (an observation, not a finding):** on the common sample its gain over persistence sits at the 7-day tenor (RMSE 4.663 vs 4.715) and it is slightly better at 14 days (2.769 vs 2.776); it is **worse** than persistence at 60, 91 and 182 days (1.108 vs 1.058, 0.872 vs 0.825, 0.798 vs 0.755). A possible reason for the short-tenor gain, untested, is that a sequence model can pick up weekly expiry-cycle regularities in the roll-down; the features do not include the anchoring expiry's days-to-expiry or the day of the week.

## 7. Limitations

- Descriptive only (L-22); 8 test years; three seeds; fixed a-priori hyper-parameters; the ridge penalty at its grid edge under both labels; neural early stopping typically at early epochs (mean best epoch 7.6 MLP / 10.1 GRU under E-1, 7.5 / 6.2 under E-2; maximum 38).
- The two labels measure different objects (a roughly constant-maturity change vs a roll-down change); neither is "the" correct target. E-2 is not a single-contract fixed-strike target and does not adjust for the one-day maturity decay.
- E-2 censors 4.9 % of anchored cells and excludes cells interpolated in maturity; its eligible sample differs from E-1's (hence the common-sample comparison). Interpolated and sparse-quote cells are excluded from the primary metric.
- Everything in L-23 to L-25 still applies: closing-snapshot mids, American options priced with a European model, parity-implied forwards, one underlying and vendor, unverified upstream licence.
- Numbers were read from the Kaggle log (plain-text tables) and are reproducible by re-running the notebook; the saved output files are on the owner's Kaggle account (private), not in Git.

## 8. What would be worth doing next (not done)

1. A **maturity-adjusted fixed-expiry** label (roll the same expiry to constant maturity by total variance) to separate roll-down from genuine surface dynamics.
2. Add the **anchoring expiry's days-to-expiry and the weekday** to the features so models can learn the deterministic roll-down.
3. Only then revisit model comparisons, with a validated inferential method if one ever exists (it does not today).
